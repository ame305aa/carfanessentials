Help and support

The circle is also the people.

A frankincense business that only buys sacks and sells bottles leaves the grove and the city poorer. Carfan is built the other way: families tap, neighbors finish, and the trees are given rest.

Harvester families

We buy tears directly from the people who cut them. The price is agreed before the season, in a conversation, not after the sack is on our table. Groves are named. A lot we cannot trace does not enter the still.

The Jigjiga bench

Sorting, filling, and packing stay in the city. Those are jobs for neighbors, not tasks sent away. A young person can learn the table, then the still, then the label, without leaving home to find the work.

Neighbors around the workshop

When a production season closes, Carfan sets aside frankincense soap and school kits for families near the workshop. The support is physical and local. It is not a slogan on the carton.

A living tree

Support starts by not using the grove up.

The most useful thing we can do for a tapping family is leave the trees able to give again. Shallow cuts. A rest after a tapping. No second rush cut because a buyer is impatient. The oil can wait. The tree cannot be replaced in a season.

What we practice

Five supports, kept simple enough to keep.

Price

The price of resin is spoken and agreed before tapping starts. A family knows the season before the first cut. We do not renegotiate downward when the tears arrive.

Craft

Young tappers learn beside someone who already knows the trees. In Jigjiga, the same patience is used at the sorting table and the packing bench.

Soap circle

Women in Jigjiga who want the craft can use the soap bench, the molds, and the resin infusion. They learn the pour. A Carfan batch stays a Carfan batch. A batch they make to take home is theirs.

Season close

At the end of a production season, soap from that season and school kits are set aside for families around the workshop. If you live nearby and need to ask, call the bench.

Return

Bring an empty Carfan oil bottle back. When that oil is in the still again, we refill it. Less new glass, and a reason to see the workshop.

Who this is for

It is for the family that keeps a grove and should not have to sell the tears as if they were nameless. It is for the neighbor who can sort, fill, and pack without leaving Jigjiga. It is for the woman who wants a soap skill she can carry. It is for the household that wants an oil, a cream, a bar, or a shampoo made in the same city.
